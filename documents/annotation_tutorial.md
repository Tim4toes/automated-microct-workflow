# User Guide: Volumetric Micro-CT Segmentation Tool

Welcome! This guide will walk you through how to use the **Volumetric Micro-CT Segmentation Tool** from start to finish. Even if you have never used a Python program before, you can follow these step-by-step instructions to isolate bone structures and create clean, 1-bit monochrome mask files ready for **CTAn**.

To make every tool easy to understand, we will use a **100-slice example dataset (Slices 1 to 100)** throughout this guide.

---

## 1. Understanding the Two Windows

When you start the program, two windows will appear on your screen:

1. **`Controls Menu` Window**: A clean reference table listing every mouse action and keyboard shortcut. Keep this open on the side of your monitor for quick reference.
2. **`Volumetric CC Editor` Window**: Your main workspace where you view and edit your micro-CT bone slices.
   * *Note on the Cursor*: Inside this window, your mouse pointer will automatically turn into a **crosshair (+)**. This helps you place pixel-accurate cuts and bridges.
   * *Important Tip*: Before pressing any keyboard shortcut, make sure you **click once on the `Volumetric CC Editor` window** so your computer knows you are typing into the viewer (and not into another background program).

### Reading the Status Text (Top-Left Corner)
At the top-left of your main viewer, you will see two lines of text:

* **Line 1**: `[1/100] slice_0001.tif | BOUNDS: 1 to 100 | Rev: NO | Mode: OVERLAY`
  * **`[1/100]`**: Tells you which slice you are currently viewing out of the total stack (e.g., Slice 1 of 100).
  * **`BOUNDS: 1 to 100`**: Shows which slices will be actively processed during a batch export.
  * **`Rev: NO`**: Indicates if the dataset is currently in its normal Z-direction (`NO`) or reversed top-to-bottom (`YES`). 
  * **`Mode: OVERLAY` / `MASK` / `[INTERP]`**: Shows your current viewing mode (and displays `[INTERP]` if the current slice was generated by rigid 3D shape interpolation).
  * **Why does Line 1 turn RED?** Line 1 is normally **white**. Whenever a slice contains a manual **Scalpel Cut**, a **Bone Bridge**, or an **Interpolated Shape**, Line 1 automatically turns **RED** so you can immediately tell the slice has been modified.

* **Line 2**: `Live Dil: 0px | Export Dil: 1px | Locked: True | Persist: OFF`
  * Displays your current dilation settings, whether your settings are safety-locked (`Locked: True`), and whether Persistent Drawing Mode (`Persist: OFF`) is active.

## 2. Step-by-Step Guide & Examples (Using a 100-Slice Dataset)

Imagine we have loaded a folder containing **100 micro-CT slices (Slice 1 to Slice 100)**. Below is how each function works in real-world scenarios across this stack.

---

### A. Opening Folders & Controlling the Camera

#### 1. Open a New Dataset Folder (`o` key)
* **What it does**: Opens a standard Windows folder-selection window so you can switch to a new scan without restarting the program. It also automatically clears old edits from memory so they don't mix with your new scan.
* **100-Slice Example**: You just finished working on `dataset1_sost` and want to load `dataset2_control` (which has 100 slices). Press **`o`**, click on the `dataset2_control` folder, and click **Select Folder**. Slice 1 of the new 100-slice stack appears immediately.

#### 2. Zooming In and Out (`Scroll Wheel` or `+` / `-` keys)
* **What it does**: Zooms the camera directly toward or away from your mouse cursor so you can inspect tiny bone pores or sutures.
* **100-Slice Example**: On **Slice 10**, you notice a tiny gap between two bone plates. Hover your crosshair over that gap and **scroll your mouse wheel up** (or press **`+`**) until you can clearly see individual pixels.

#### 3. Panning the Camera (`Middle Click & Drag`)
* **What it does**: Slides the image around when you are zoomed in.
* **100-Slice Example**: While zoomed in closely on **Slice 10**, press and hold your **Mouse Scroll Wheel down (Middle Click)** and drag the mouse left or right to follow the edge of the bone plate.

#### 4. Reset Camera View (`r` key)
* **What it does**: Instantly re-centers the bone scan and fits it neatly inside your window. Also use this after resizing the window with your mouse to snap the image cleanly to the new window borders.
* **100-Slice Example**: After zooming in 20 times on **Slice 10**, you want to see the whole bone again. Press **`r`** and the full slice snaps back into view.

#### 5. Toggle Pure Mask View (`m` key)
* **What it does**: Switches between **OVERLAY Mode** (where your selected bone is tinted translucent red over the grayscale X-ray) and **MASK Mode** (which shows the pure black-and-white binary image that CTAn will receive).
* **100-Slice Example**: On **Slice 12**, you want to verify that all internal pores inside your bone plate were completely filled in. Press **`m`** to view the solid white mask against a pure black background. Press **`m`** again to return to the red translucent overlay.

---

### B. Navigating Slices & Setting Boundaries

#### 1. Next Slice (`j` key) & Previous Slice (`h` key) with Auto-Propagation
* **What it does**: Moves forward (`j`) or backward (`h`) one slice at a time. As you move, the program automatically looks at where your red mask was on the previous slice and selects the overlapping bone on the new slice!
* **100-Slice Example**: After selecting your target bone on **Slice 10**, press **`j`** five times to step through **Slices 11, 12, 13, 14, and 15**. Even though the bone shifts and changes shape slightly from slice to slice, the program automatically tracks and highlights your target bone on each slice without you having to click again. If you go one slice too far, press **`h`** to step back.

#### 2. Flip Z-Direction (`f` key)
* **What it does**: Instantly reverses the physical order of the dataset in memory (e.g., Slice 100 becomes the "first" slice, and Slice 1 becomes the "last" slice). Because tools like auto-propagation (`j`) and shape interpolation (`i`) are mathematically designed to build structures *forward* through the stack, pressing `f` allows you to work backwards top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top effortlessly. 
* **100-Slice Example**: You open the dataset and realize the complex bone structure is much easier to isolate if you start from the bottom at Slice 100. Instead of trying to run interpolation backwards (which won't work), you simply press **`f`**. 
  * The physical **Slice 100** instantly appears on your screen as `[1/100]`, and the top bar says `Rev: YES`. 
  * You can now safely use `j`, `p` (Persist), and `i` (Interpolate) exactly as you normally would, moving "forward" from 100 down to 1! 
  * *Note: When you finally batch export (`e`), the `.bmp` files retain their original filenames, so CTAn still loads them in perfect alphabetical order.*

#### 3. Set Start Boundary (`v` key) & Set End Boundary (`b` key)
* **What it does**: Tells the program where your Region of Interest (ROI) starts (`v`) and ends (`b`). During batch export, any slices outside this range are automatically saved as blank (black) masks so your slice numbering stays 100% aligned with CTAn.
* **100-Slice Example**: In your 100-slice scan, **Slices 1 to 9** and **Slices 91 to 100** are outside your target anatomical landmark.
  1. Navigate to **Slice 10** and press **`v`**. The top status bar updates to `BOUNDS: 10 to 100`.
  2. Navigate to **Slice 90** and press **`b`**. The top status bar updates to `BOUNDS: 10 to 90`.
  3. Now, when you export, only Slices 10 through 90 will contain bone masks. Slices 1–9 and 91–100 will be perfectly blank files.

#### 4. Reset Boundaries (`x` key)
* **What it does**: Resets the start and end boundaries back to the full stack.

---

### C. Manual Editing Tools: Scalpel Cuts & Bone Bridges

By default, the program uses an automatic density detector (**Otsu thresholding**) to find all bright bone structures. However, sometimes the raw micro-CT scan has imperfections.

#### 1. Draw a Scalpel Cut (`Right Click & Drag`)
* **What it does**: Draws a thin, 1-pixel **white line** (at 20% opacity) that acts like a surgical scalpel, cutting apart two touching bone structures so they can be selected independently.
* **100-Slice Example**: You press `j` to move from Slice 15 to **Slice 16**. On **Slice 16**, a thin suture bridges your target bone to a neighboring bone plate, and suddenly both bones turn red!
  1. Zoom in on the suture using the **Scroll Wheel**.
  2. Hold down **Right Click** and drag a white line completely across the narrow bridge where the two bones touch.
  3. The program immediately recalculates: the two bones are now separated! **Left Click** the unwanted neighboring bone to unselect it, leaving only your target bone highlighted in red. Notice the top status text turns **RED** to indicate **Slice 16** now has a manual edit.

#### 2. Draw a Bone Bridge (`Shift + Right Click & Drag`)
* **What it does**: Draws a thin, 1-pixel **green line** (at 20% opacity) that acts as an artificial bone wall. If a porous pocket inside your bone has a small opening to the black background, sealing that opening with a green bridge tells the program to automatically fill the trapped dark pocket as part of your red ROI.
* **100-Slice Example**: On **Slice 22**, you notice a dark marrow cavity near the edge of your bone that is *not* highlighted in red because a tiny crack connects that cavity to the outside background.
  1. Hold down the **`Shift`** key on your keyboard, then **Right Click & Drag** a green line across the mouth of the crack to connect the two sides of the bone wall.
  2. As soon as you let go of the mouse button, the green bridge seals the opening, and the entire dark cavity inside automatically fills with red!

#### 3. Undo Cuts & Bridges Only (`u` key) vs. Clear Entire Slice (`c` key)
* **What it does**:
  * **`u` key (Undo Lines)**: Removes all white scalpel lines and green bridge lines on the current slice, **while keeping your bone selected**.
  * **`c` key (Clear All)**: Wipes the current slice completely clean—removing all scalpel lines, bridge lines, **and** unselecting all bones.

---

### D. Speed Tools for Difficult Regions: Persistent Mode (`p`) vs. Interpolation (`i`)

When a tricky suture or fragmented structure spans across dozens of slices, you have two ways to handle it quickly without redrawing from scratch on every single slice:

#### 1. Persistent Edits Mode (`p` key)
* **What it does**: When toggled ON (`Persist: ON`), any scalpel cuts or bridge lines on your current slice are automatically copied (and merged) onto the next slice as you scroll forward. The automatic Otsu bone-finder still runs on every slice, dynamically hugging the true physical bone boundaries right up against your carried-over barrier wall.
* **100-Slice Example**: From **Slice 25 to Slice 35**, two bone plates touch at the exact same spot across all 11 slices.
  1. On **Slice 25**, press **`p`**. Look at Line 2 of the status bar to confirm it says **`Persist: ON`**.
  2. Draw your white scalpel cut across the suture on **Slice 25**.
  3. Press **`j`** repeatedly to step from **Slice 26 up to Slice 35**. On every single slice, your scalpel line is automatically pasted in place, keeping the two bones cleanly separated!
  4. Once you reach **Slice 36** where the bones naturally separate on their own, press **`p`** again to turn **`Persist: OFF`**.

#### 2. 3D Shape Interpolation (`i` key)
* **What it does**: Sometimes a span of slices is so intricate, porous, or noisy that you want to bypass the dynamic Otsu detector entirely. Pressing **`i`** takes your approved red mask on your previous manual keyframe (e.g., Slice 40) and your approved red mask on your current slice (e.g., Slice 60), and builds a rigid, smooth, CTAn-style **3D geometric tunnel** connecting them across Slices 41–59. This hardcoded tunnel overrides the raw scan density.
* **100-Slice Example**:
  1. You process **Slices 10 to 39** normally using the automatic Otsu method.
  2. On **Slice 40**, you draw a scalpel cut and **Left Click** the bone to ensure your red mask looks exactly the way you want. (By editing Slice 40, the program remembers it as a manual **keyframe**).
  3. Next, press **`j`** to scroll rapidly ahead to **Slice 60** (don't worry about what happens on Slices 41–59 while scrolling past them).
  4. On **Slice 60**, draw your cut/bridge and make sure your target bone is highlighted in red.
  5. Press **`i`**.
  6. The program prints `Interpolating rigid tunnel from slice 40 to 60...` and deletes any legacy lines inside the gap, generating solid shapes for **Slices 41 through 59**.
  7. If you press **`h`** to scroll backward and inspect **Slices 41–59**, you will see `Mode: OVERLAY [INTERP]` in the status text.
  8. **What happens on Slice 61?** When you move forward from Slice 60 to **Slice 61** (`j`), the program automatically resumes normal Otsu thresholding!
  9. **What if an interpolated slice needs a fix?** You can scroll back to **Slice 45** at any time. The moment you **Left Click** or draw a cut/bridge on Slice 45, it immediately abandons the hardcoded tunnel, snaps back to physical Otsu boundaries with your new edit applied, and becomes a brand-new keyframe!

### E. Parameter Tuning & Safety Lock (`l`, `[`, `]`, `{`, `}` keys)

Dilation expands the outer boundary of a mask by 1 pixel per step. The program separates dilation into two types: **Live Dilation** (used while finding connected bones) and **Export Dilation** (applied only at the very end when saving `.bmp` files).

#### 1. Lock / Unlock Parameter Tuning (`l` key)
* **What it does**: By default, `Locked: True` is shown in **yellow text** on Line 2 so you cannot accidentally bump the bracket keys (`[`, `]`, `{`, `}`) while annotating. Pressing **`l`** unlocks the parameters (`Locked: False` in white text) so you can adjust them.

#### 2. Decrease / Increase LIVE Dilation (`[` and `]` keys)
* **What it does**: Expands the raw thresholded bone *before* the program searches for connected components. Usually best kept at `0px`, unless your bone is severely speckled and broken into tiny disconnected islands.

#### 3. Decrease / Increase EXPORT Dilation (`{` and `}` keys)
* **What it does**: Expands your final approved mask outward by $1\text{ px}$ per iteration *only when saving `.bmp` files to disk*. This is useful if the raw Otsu threshold sits slightly inside the visible edge of the bone, and you want the final CTAn mask to have a 1-pixel buffer without causing neighbouring bones to merge while you are working.

---

### F. Saving Your Work & Exporting Masks for CTAn (`w`, `a`, `s`, `e`, `q` keys)

#### 1. Save Workspace Memory (`w` key) & Load Workspace Memory (`a` key)
* **What it does**:
  * **`w` key**: Compresses and saves all of your approved masks, scalpel cuts, green bridges, keyframes, interpolated slices, reverse direction, and bounds into a tiny `.npz` file inside your output folder.
  * **`a` key**: Loads that `.npz` file back into memory so you can continue exactly where you left off.

#### 2. Save Current Single Slice Only (`s` key)
* **What it does**: Exports *only* the slice you are currently viewing as a 1-bit monochrome `.bmp` file into your output folder.

#### 3. Batch Export Entire Volume (`e` key)
* **What it does**: Processes your entire 100-slice dataset from start to finish and saves every slice as a **1-bit monochrome `.bmp` file** ready for CTAn.
  * Slices outside your `BOUNDS` (set by `v` and `b`) are saved as pure black 1-bit images.
  * Slices you manually approved or interpolated use your exact saved masks.
  * Any unvisited slices inside your bounds automatically propagate forward from the last valid mask.

#### 4. Quit the Program (`q` key, `Esc` key, or Window `X` Button)
* **What it does**: Safely closes both the viewer and the controls window. Always remember to press **`w`** before quitting if you want to save your session!

## 3. Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

| Input / Shortcut | Action | When to Use It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Left Click** | Select / Unselect bone | Click on a bone plate to toggle its red mask on or off. |
| **Right Click & Drag** | Draw **Scalpel** (White) | Sever a narrow suture where two separate bones touch. |
| **Shift + Right Click** | Draw **Bridge** (Green) | Seal an open crack on a bone edge so internal dark pores fill with red. |
| **Scroll Wheel** / **`+`** **`-`** | Zoom In / Out | Inspect fine trabecular details or pixel boundaries. |
| **Middle Click & Drag** | Pan Camera | Move around the image while zoomed in. |
| **`r`** | Reset View | Fit and center the image back inside the window. |
| **`j`** / **`h`** | Next / Previous Slice | Step forward (`j`) or backward (`h`) and auto-propagate your selection. |
| **`p`** | Toggle Persistent Edits | Copy your cuts/bridges automatically to the next slice as you scroll forward. |
| **`i`** | Interpolate Regions | Build a rigid 3D tunnel between your previous keyframe and current slice. |
| **`f`** | Flip Z-Direction | Reverse the physical dataset processing order to work bottom-to-top. |
| **`u`** | Undo Cuts & Bridges | Erase drawn lines on the current slice while keeping the bone selected. |
| **`c`** | Clear Slice Completely | Erase all drawn lines *and* unselect all bones on the current slice. |
| **`v`** / **`b`** | Set Start / End Bound | Restrict mask export to slices between Start (`v`) and End (`b`). |
| **`x`** | Reset Bounds | Reset export boundaries back to the full stack. |
| **`m`** | Toggle Mask View | Preview the pure black-and-white binary mask without the X-ray background. |
| **`l`** | Lock / Unlock Parameters | Unlock (`False`) before changing dilation; Lock (`True`) to prevent accidents. |
| **`[`** / **`]`** | Live Dilation ($-1$ / $+1\text{ px}$) | Expand thresholded bone during live selection (requires `Locked: False`). |
| **`{`** / **`}`** | Export Dilation ($-1$ / $+1\text{ px}$) | Expand final mask during `.bmp` export only (requires `Locked: False`). |
| **`w`** | Save Workspace | Save all masks, cuts, bridges, and keyframes to a compact `.npz` file. |
| **`a`** | Load Workspace | Restore a previously saved `.npz` workspace session. |
| **`s`** | Save Single Slice | Export only the currently viewed slice as a 1-bit monochrome `.bmp`. |
| **`e`** | Batch Export Volume | Export all slices in the dataset as 1-bit monochrome `.bmp` files for CTAn. |
| **`o`** | Open Dataset Folder | Switch to a different folder of micro-CT images. |
| **`q`** or **`Esc`** | Quit | Close the program. |
